# OpenShore Community Guidelines for Model Reviews

DRAFT FOR COUNSEL REVIEW. Not in effect. Prepared 2026-09-24.

Effective date: [EFFECTIVE DATE]

Model reviews help people pick a model that will actually run on the machine
they have. They only work if they are honest. These guidelines say what belongs
in a review, what does not, and what happens when something goes wrong. You
accept them before you post your first review. The Terms of Use apply as well.

## 1. What a review is

A review is a report of a run. You ran a model, on hardware you name, and you
tell others how it went.

Each review can hold: stars, your text (up to 2,000 characters), what you used
the model for, your hardware and memory, the speed you saw, the quantization,
and a display name. Your display name and your review are public. Your account
id is never shown.

## 2. What a good review looks like

- **You ran it.** On your own machine, or one you control.
- **It is specific.** Name the task. Say what worked and what did not.
- **The numbers are real.** Report the speed you measured, on the hardware you
  listed, at the quantization you used. Do not round up.
- **The stars match the words.** A three-star review should read like one.
- **It is about the model.** Not the person who made it, and not other
  reviewers.

A short honest review beats a long glowing one.

## 3. What is not allowed

- **Fake reviews.** A review of a run that did not happen, a review written
  for someone else, or a review posted under a made-up identity.
- **Reviews of a model you did not run.** Even if you read a lot about it.
- **Paid or rewarded reviews without disclosure.** If anyone gave you money,
  credits, free compute, or anything else of value for the review, or for a
  positive one, you may not post it. If you got something of value that was not
  tied to the review, say so plainly at the top of the review.
- **Undisclosed conflicts of interest.** If you work for, are paid by, or own a
  stake in the model's maker, or a competitor, say so at the top of the review.
  Do not review your own model to push its rating up, or a competitor's to push
  it down.
- **Personal data.** Names, emails, addresses, phone numbers, or other details
  that identify someone, including yourself beyond your display name.
- **Harassment.** Insults, threats, slurs, or attacks on a person or a group.
- **Impersonation.** A display name that pretends to be another person or a
  company you do not speak for.
- **Illegal content.** Anything unlawful, including anything the Terms of Use
  prohibit. Child sexual abuse material is removed and reported to the National
  Center for Missing and Exploited Children.
- **Spam.** Ads, links to sell something, repeated reviews, or text unrelated
  to the model.
- **Copying.** Someone else's review, article, or benchmark posted as your own.

## 4. What OpenShore does, and does not do

We hold ourselves to the same rules. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and
testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) applies to us, and we follow it.

- **We never write fake reviews**, and we never pay for reviews or reward
  positive ones.
- **We never remove a review because it is negative.** We remove a review only
  when it breaks these guidelines, and we apply them the same way to praise and
  to criticism.
- **Staff reviews are labeled.** A review by an OpenShore employee or the
  founder carries an "OpenShore team" label. The label is set by our server, so
  it cannot be left off.
- **The rating is the plain average.** We show the raw average of the stars with
  the number of reviews next to it. We show no average until a model has at
  least five reviews.
- **Our benchmark is shown apart.** The OpenShore benchmark fit is our own
  measurement. It appears separately, with its own label, and it never changes
  the community average.

## 5. Your reviews

You can edit or delete your review at any time while it is visible. A review
that has been hidden after reports cannot be edited until we have looked at it.

Deleting your account deletes your reviews.

## 6. Reporting and blocking

**Report.** Any signed-in person can report a review that breaks these
guidelines. Each person can report a given review once.

**Auto-hide.** After reports from several different people, a review is hidden
automatically until we have looked at it.

**The 24-hour look.** We look at every reported review within 24 hours. We then
restore it, or remove it. If a review is restored after a look, its earlier
reports do not hide it again.

**Block.** You can block a reviewer. Their reviews no longer appear for you.
Your block list is private to you.

**Accounts.** Repeated or serious breaks of these guidelines can lead to losing
the ability to post reviews, or to losing the account.

## 7. Appeals

If we removed your review or acted on your account and you think we got it
wrong, write to os-code@openshorellc.com. Include the model and your display
name, and tell us why. We will look again and reply within
[APPEAL RESPONSE TIME].

## 8. Copyright

If you believe a review copies your work without permission, send a notice to
os-code@openshorellc.com. A dedicated copyright address is coming. Include:

1. Your name, address, phone number, and email.
2. The work you believe was copied.
3. The review that copies it, with enough detail for us to find it.
4. A statement that you believe in good faith the use is not authorized by
   you, your agent, or the law.
5. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that your notice is accurate and that
   you own the right or are authorized to act for its owner.
6. Your physical or electronic signature.

If your review was removed after a notice and you believe that was a mistake,
you can send a counter-notice to the same address. We will explain what it must
contain when we tell you about the removal.

We will register a designated agent with the US Copyright Office. Until then,
notices go to the email above or by mail to Open Shore, LLC,
[MAILING ADDRESS].

We remove the accounts of people who repeatedly infringe.
