OpenShore · Executive Brief

Build an entire app, on your own machines, private the whole way.

OpenShore is a native coding companion for Linux desktop and iPhone that closes the entire app-building loop into one private circuit: local models do the thinking, an agent writes and runs the code, your repositories and notes live on storage you own, the work follows you from desk to phone over your own network, and you ship to the App Store or Google Play from inside the app. Cloud stays one deliberate tap away, on your own keys. What makes it different

Local first

Your models run on your hardware, not someone else's cloud.

A pocket model on the iPhone, big models on the desktop, reached over your own network. On a plane, it still works.

Your stack

One reasoning model runs the show and routes every task.

Set the quarterback, place specialists by category. It sends each task to the right model and covers what you have not placed.

Own your substrate

Your code and notes live where you choose.

Repositories and an Obsidian-compatible Vault on storage you control (local, iCloud, Drive), encrypted, portable, no vendor in the middle.

Launch

Take a finished build to the stores from inside the app.

The model walks the setup and reads the build result, so a failure comes back as a fix, not a wall of logs.

Private by construction

Sealed on your device, answering only to you.

Everything encrypted at rest with AES-256. Cloud sees only the calls you choose, on your own keys. No telemetry, no analytics, no phone-home, ever. Where it can be, privacy is measured on your machine, not asserted.

Why the arc is the moat

A fast 2026 wave (Paseo, OpenMono, and others) is racing for the same "sovereign, cross-device" corner. Each owns one slice: Paseo owns the remote control but orchestrates cloud brains; OpenMono owns the local agent but is a terminal tool and a marketing funnel; the local runners own compute but stop at chat; the cloud agents own polish but rent the brains and never leave the desk.

OpenShore owns the whole loop, idea to shipped app, native and finished, with the code never leaving your machines. Any rival can copy one box on the strip. Becoming the entire strip is the thing none of them can do without becoming a different company. The moat is not "local AI." It is the closed loop, executed to product finish.
